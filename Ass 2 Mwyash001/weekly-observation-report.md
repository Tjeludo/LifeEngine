# Weekly Observation Report

## Core observation

During the short repeated observations, complete extinction was not seen: the rhino, leopard, grass, and bush remained visible. The main correlation was between local food access and animal activity. Green producer cells created blue food, and the rhino was more likely to survive or reproduce when food was nearby. The leopard began farther away, so predation was delayed at first. A possible cause of later population decline is that nearby food becomes depleted before plants can replace it, leaving the rhino exposed to the leopard. One theory is that spatial separation creates temporary stability rather than real adaptation. Another theory is that a plant population could expand into crowded areas and make reproduction harder. These theories cannot yet be confirmed because this was a short observation period and no complete extinction occurred.
