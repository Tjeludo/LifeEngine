# Weekly Observation Report

## Core observation

Across repeated observations, complete extinction was not consistently seen. Rhinos, leopards, grass, and bushes generally remained present, although their numbers and locations changed. A clear correlation was the link between rhino survival and the amount of food produced by grass and bushes. The likely cause is that rhinos eat blue food and reproduce, while leopard killer cells reduce rhino survival when the species meet. This creates a predator-prey relationship rather than simple failure to adapt. One theory is that a successful rhino population could attract more leopards, causing evolutionary suicide if predation later becomes stronger than reproduction. Another theory is that plants could spread widely and change the space available for animals. These are possible explanations, not proven causes, because the repeated observations did not record a complete extinction.

## Further interpretation

MTSVIM006 focuses on the balance between predator growth and prey recovery. Longer trials with repeated starting conditions would show whether leopard pressure consistently follows rhino population growth.
